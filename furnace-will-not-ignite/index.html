    Hot Surface Ignitor Failure: The Most Common Cause Across Ontario

    A failed hot surface ignitor is the most frequent reason a gas furnace will not produce heat. The ignitor is a small resistive element that glows at temperatures over 1,800 degrees Fahrenheit to light the gas burners, and it degrades gradually over its operating life until it can no longer reach the temperature needed to ignite reliably. Most ignitors last four to seven years under normal residential use, making this a predictable maintenance item that is also one of the more straightforward Furnace Will Not Ignite Ontario repairs our team handles.

    The furnace typically goes through the full startup sequence when the ignitor has failed: the inducer runs, the pressure switch closes, and the gas valve opens, but the burners never light because nothing is hot enough to ignite the gas. After two or three failed attempts, the control board locks out the system as a safety measure.

    How to Recognize a Failed Ignitor in Ontario

    The most reliable sign of an ignitor failure is a furnace that clicks, runs its inducer motor, and then shuts down without ever producing warmth. You may hear the startup sequence begin normally and then nothing. Some homeowners across Ontario can see the ignitor glow orange through the furnace's inspection window; if it does not glow at all or glows weakly before the burners fail to light, the ignitor is the most likely culprit. With the ignitor replaced and the system properly serviced, a gas furnace in otherwise sound condition can deliver reliable heat for many more years.

    Dirty Flame Sensor Causing Furnace Will Not Ignite Ontario in Your Home

    A dirty flame sensor produces symptoms almost identical to a failed ignitor. The sensor is a small metal rod that detects the presence of a flame through electrical conductance, and when oxide buildup coats the rod, it cannot sense the flame accurately even when the burners have lit. The furnace ignites briefly and then shuts down within a second or two, repeating this cycle a few times before locking out entirely.

    Furnace Will Not Ignite Ontario situations where the flame sensor is the cause are often resolved with a careful cleaning rather than a full replacement. Our technicians check the sensor's microamp reading during every ignition diagnostic to determine whether cleaning will restore proper function or whether the sensor has degraded beyond recovery. Addressing this part during annual service prevents it from becoming the reason a homeowner wakes up to a cold house.

    Draft Inducer Motor and Pressure Switch Faults in Ontario

    The draft inducer motor evacuates combustion gases from the heat exchanger before the ignition sequence begins. When it runs slowly, fails to start, or develops bearing noise that reduces its output, the pressure switch does not close and the furnace control board never advances past the pre-purge stage. Homeowners across Ontario hear the inducer run briefly or not at all, with the furnace then going silent without any attempt at ignition.

    Pressure switch faults can also originate from a cracked vacuum hose, a blocked condensate drain, or a switch diaphragm that has worn out over time. Our team checks the inducer RPM, the vacuum port, and the switch itself before concluding which part is responsible. These are separate failure modes that look similar from the outside.

    Gas Valve and Supply Problems Behind Furnace Will Not Ignite Ontario

    A gas valve that is not opening correctly can prevent ignition even when the ignitor and flame sensor are both functioning properly. The ignitor glows, the control board sends the signal to open the valve, and nothing ignites because no gas is flowing through the burners. Most Furnace Will Not Ignite Ontario cases involving the gas valve show a healthy ignitor glow with no combustion, which is a clear indicator that the fuel side of the system needs attention.

    Gas supply issues are less common but worth checking early in the diagnostic. A main shutoff that was accidentally turned off during a renovation, a gas meter issue, or a supply disruption affecting the street can produce the same symptom as a faulty gas valve inside the furnace. Our technicians verify supply pressure at the appliance before assuming the valve has failed, which prevents an unnecessary part replacement on a system that was simply not receiving gas.

    Control Board Faults That Prevent Ignition Across Ontario

    The control board manages the entire ignition sequence. When it fails, symptoms can appear at any stage of the startup process: the inducer may not run, the ignitor may not receive power, the gas valve signal may not be sent, or the board may lock out after one cycle without a clear reason. Furnace Will Not Ignite Ontario problems that trace back to the control board are among the more complex diagnoses because the board interacts with every other component.

    Our approach is to eliminate the more common failure points first. If the ignitor, flame sensor, inducer, and pressure switch all test correctly but the furnace still will not complete an ignition cycle, the control board becomes the primary focus. Replacing a board without confirming all other components are functioning correctly can result in a new board failing quickly from an underlying issue that was never addressed.

    When a furnace will not ignite, the cause is usually one of a handful of well-known components. Here are the problems our clients call about most.

    🔥

    Furnace Ignitor Has Failed

    The hot surface ignitor is a fragile ceramic element that glows red-hot to light the burners. Over time it becomes brittle and cracks, breaking the ignition circuit entirely. We test ignitor resistance and replace failed units with compatible parts to restore proper startup.

    ⚡

    Furnace Flame Sensor Dirty or Failing

    The flame sensor reads whether the burners are lit and shuts the gas valve if it detects no flame. A layer of oxidation on the sensor rod causes false readings, so the furnace lights briefly and then shuts off within seconds. Cleaning or replacing the sensor restores reliable flame detection and keeps the burners running.

    🔒

    Furnace Stuck in Safety Lockout

    After two or three failed ignition attempts, most furnaces lock out automatically to prevent gas buildup. The lockout light or error code on the control board indicates the system needs a manual reset before it will try again. A technician clears the fault, identifies the root cause, and confirms the ignition sequence completes successfully before leaving.

    🔧

    Furnace Gas Valve Not Opening

    If the ignitor glows normally but no flame appears, the gas valve may not be opening to allow fuel to reach the burners. A failed valve coil or a control board not sending the correct signal are both common causes. We test the valve electrically and mechanically to confirm whether it needs replacement.

    💨

    Furnace Inducer Motor Not Starting

    The draft inducer motor must reach full speed before the control board allows the ignition sequence to begin. A motor that is slow, noisy, or intermittent causes the furnace to stall before the ignitor ever fires. Replacing a worn inducer motor restores the proper pressure conditions the system needs to light safely.

    🌫️

    Furnace Filter Blocking Airflow

    A severely blocked air filter prevents enough airflow across the heat exchanger, causing it to overheat and trip the high-limit switch. Once the limit trips, the control board shuts down the ignition sequence as a safety measure. Replacing the filter and resetting the limit switch typically resolves this issue without any parts replacement.

    🖥️

    Furnace Control Board Not Sending Signal

    The furnace control board coordinates every step of the startup sequence, including sending voltage to the ignitor and opening the gas valve. A burned relay or cracked solder joint on the board can interrupt this signal without producing any visible damage. We test each output from the board to confirm whether it needs repair or replacement.

    🔌

    Furnace Won't Start After Power Outage

    Power surges and outages can trip internal safety switches or cause the control board to enter a fault-lockout state, leaving the furnace unresponsive even after power is restored. Resetting the furnace manually without checking for underlying voltage damage can cause the board to fail prematurely. We inspect the board, verify all safety switches have reset properly, and confirm stable operation before closing the call.

    🚨

    Gas Smell When Furnace Tries to Start

    A detectable gas odour during a startup attempt can indicate a leaking gas valve, a cracked heat exchanger allowing combustion gases to enter the air stream, or a supply line issue upstream of the furnace. This condition requires immediate attention and the home should be vacated until a licensed gas technician has assessed the system. We carry all required safety equipment and perform a full combustion and leak check before restoring the system to service.
