    What Causes Gas Fireplaces to Stop Working in Lucan

    Gas fireplaces in Lucan tend to fail in predictable ways, and the most common causes cluster around four categories of components.

    Understanding the failure category helps set the right expectations for the repair, the parts involved, and the time required to complete the work. Here is where problems typically originate:

    • Ignition and pilot system: Worn thermocouple, degraded thermopile, blocked pilot orifice, or a weak spark from a failing igniter module
    • Gas valve and pressure: Valve that no longer opens fully, gas pressure outside the operating range, or a valve that closes prematurely due to a low thermopile signal
    • Blower and electrical components: Seized blower motor, failed capacitor, corroded wiring, or a remote receiver or wall switch that has stopped communicating correctly
    • Combustion and venting: Blocked flue cap, deteriorated flex vent, disconnected pipe section, or a draft issue affecting combustion quality and sensor response

    Each of these categories produces different symptoms and requires different diagnostic steps. A pilot that will not stay lit points to the first category. A fireplace that starts and then shuts off after a few minutes usually involves the second. Our technicians work through the categories systematically so the repair addresses the right component.

    Ignition Failure and Pilot Problems Across Lucan

    Ignition and pilot problems account for the majority of gas fireplace repair calls across Lucan, and they are the most misunderstood failure category among homeowners.

    A thermocouple is a heat-sensing probe that generates a small millivolt signal when the pilot flame touches it. That signal tells the gas valve it is safe to stay open. When the thermocouple wears out, the signal drops below the valve's minimum threshold and the pilot goes out. A thermopile works on the same principle but generates a higher millivolt output, typically used to power the valve directly without any external electrical supply. Both components degrade gradually, which is why a fireplace may work on and off for several weeks before failing completely.

    Our technicians measure the output from both components during every Gas Fireplace Repair Lucan visit. A thermocouple producing less than 15 millivolts or a thermopile reading below 300 millivolts under load is flagged for replacement regardless of whether it is currently causing a visible failure. Catching early degradation during a repair visit prevents a repeat call within the same season.

    Gas fireplaces fail in predictable ways. Here are the issues our clients contact us about most, and exactly how we fix them.

    🔥

    Pilot Light Won't Stay Lit

    Almost always a thermocouple or thermopile that has degraded below the millivolt threshold required to hold the gas valve open. Once the signal drops far enough, the valve closes and the pilot goes out regardless of how many times it is relit. We test both components, measure the output under load, and replace whichever one is no longer performing to spec.

    ⚡

    Fireplace Igniter Not Working

    If you hear a clicking sound but the pilot will not light, the igniter module may be cracked, fouled with carbon deposits, or incorrectly positioned relative to the pilot orifice. A weak spark can also result from a failing igniter module that still clicks but cannot produce enough energy to ignite the gas stream. We clean, adjust, or replace igniters across all common fireplace configurations.

    🔧

    Pilot Holds But Main Burner Won't Fire

    If the pilot lights but the main burner will not come on, or the burner starts and then shuts off within seconds, the gas valve is often the cause. Valve failures are frequently misdiagnosed as thermocouple issues. We test the valve's millivolt draw and opening response before concluding replacement is necessary, and we source correct specification valves for the unit being repaired.

    💨

    Fireplace Blower Not Working or Too Noisy

    A fireplace that produces heat but does not circulate it usually has a failed blower motor, a seized bearing, or a capacitor that can no longer start the motor under load. A grinding or rattling sound typically means a bearing is failing before the motor seizes completely. We test the full blower circuit, lubricate serviceable bearings, and replace the motor assembly when required.

    🛑

    Fireplace Shuts Off Mid-Burn

    A fireplace that starts normally but shuts down after 5 to 15 minutes is usually a thermopile that generates enough initial voltage to open the valve but cannot sustain it under the thermal load of a full burn cycle. It can also point to a draft issue intermittently pulling the flame away from the thermocouple sensor. We diagnose the exact cause and repair it so the unit completes a full burn cycle reliably.

    📡

    Fireplace Remote or Wall Switch Not Responding

    Remote receivers lose their pairing, wall switch wiring corrodes at the terminals, and RF receiver modules fail over time. A fireplace that does not respond to the remote but works manually at the valve usually has a receiver or wiring issue rather than a mechanical fault. We test the full control circuit, repair corroded connections, and replace receiver modules or switch assemblies when needed.

    🪟

    Fireplace Glass Cracked or Fogged

    Cracked ceramic glass is a safety issue because it can fail under thermal stress during a burn cycle. Persistent fogging that does not clear after the first few minutes of use usually indicates a failing door gasket allowing combustion gases to circulate across the cold inner surface of the glass. We replace cracked glass panels and door gaskets using components sized correctly for the unit.

    ⚠️

    Gas Smell Near the Fireplace

    A persistent gas odour near the fireplace, especially when the unit is off, should be treated as urgent. Possible causes include a valve seat no longer sealing fully, a connection that has worked loose, or a flexible gas connector that has developed a pinhole. Do not attempt to use the fireplace. Turn off the gas supply at the shutoff and call us at (855) 940-4665 to arrange an immediate inspection.

    🌡️

    Fireplace Not Producing Enough Heat

    A fireplace that runs but no longer warms the room the way it used to usually has a blower motor that has lost capacity, burner ports that are partially blocked, or a gas pressure issue reducing flame output below its rated level. The decline is often gradual enough that homeowners attribute it to the weather rather than the appliance. We inspect blower performance, burner condition, and gas pressure together to identify the cause and restore full heat output.
