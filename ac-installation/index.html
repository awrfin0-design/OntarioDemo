    What Installation Day Actually Looks Like For Ontario Homeowners

    A central AC installation with Ontario Service Pros follows a clear sequence from start to finish. When our technicians arrive, we begin with a brief review of the access points, condenser pad location, and electrical panel before any equipment is moved into position. The outdoor condenser is set and levelled, the indoor evaporator coil is installed above the furnace or air handler, and the refrigerant line set is run between the two units through the most direct and protected path available.

    Once the mechanical connections are made, our team completes the electrical wiring, installs the disconnect box at the outdoor unit, and performs a vacuum on the refrigerant circuit before releasing the charge. That vacuum step is what confirms there are no leaks or moisture in the system before the refrigerant is introduced. The final step is a full commissioning cycle: we run the system, check supply and return air temperatures, verify thermostat response, and confirm that the safety controls operate correctly. For Ontario homeowners, the expectation is a system that is fully tested and confirmed before we pack up, not one that is simply connected and left to run.

    Every home presents its own cooling challenge. Here are the situations our clients bring to us most often and how we handle them.

    ❄️

    Old System No Longer Cools Properly

    A system older than 15 years gradually loses capacity and efficiency, often unable to hold the set temperature on the hottest days. Running a failing unit continuously strains the compressor and drives up energy costs without improving comfort. We assess the existing system and recommend a properly sized replacement that actually matches your home's cooling load.

    🌡️

    New Unit Runs But Does Not Cool Evenly

    Uneven cooling after installation is usually caused by incorrect system sizing, poor duct condition, or an installation that skipped the load calculation step. Hot spots on upper floors or in sun-exposed rooms signal that airflow distribution was never properly balanced. We size every system to the home and check duct condition before the new unit goes in.

    🏠

    Home Has No Ductwork For Central Air

    Older homes and additions often lack the ductwork needed for a traditional central AC system, making standard installation impractical. Attempting to install central air without proper ducts results in expensive retrofits and poor performance. We assess the property and recommend ductless mini-split options that deliver reliable cooling without requiring new ductwork.

    🔄

    New System Short-Cycling Or Running Constantly

    Short cycling happens when an oversized unit cools the space too quickly and shuts off before the air is properly dehumidified, while a unit running without stopping is typically undersized for the space. Both problems trace back to skipping a proper load calculation before selecting equipment. We calculate the correct tonnage for each home before recommending any system.

    🔧

    Upper Floors Impossible To Cool

    Upper floors in multi-storey homes are typically harder to cool because heat rises and attic proximity increases the load. A single central unit without zone control or supplemental cooling for upper areas often cannot overcome that difference. We recommend the right combination of system size, duct balancing, or supplemental ductless units to solve upper-floor comfort issues.

    ⚡

    High Energy Bills After New AC Install

    Elevated energy bills after a new installation usually point to incorrect sizing, refrigerant undercharge, or leaking ductwork that forces the system to work harder than it should. These issues are often invisible during a casual installation but show up immediately on the first utility statement. We commission every system with refrigerant pressure checks and airflow verification before the job is complete.

    📡

    Smart Thermostat Not Working With New AC

    Compatibility mismatches between new AC equipment and existing smart thermostats are common when installation is done without checking wiring and communication protocols first. The result is a thermostat that cannot control the system on schedule or reads temperatures incorrectly. We confirm compatibility during installation and configure the control system to work correctly with the new equipment from day one.

    💦

    AC Installed But House Still Gets Humid

    An air conditioner that cools but leaves the home feeling muggy is usually oversized, meaning it reaches the set temperature too quickly to remove moisture from the air during a proper cycle. Poor duct sealing can also cause humid outdoor air to mix with conditioned air before it reaches the living space. We assess sizing and duct integrity together so the installed system handles both temperature and humidity properly.

    📋

    New AC Not Covered Under Warranty

    AC installations in require ESA electrical notification and compliance with TSSA refrigerant regulations, and skipping these steps can void equipment warranties and create issues when selling the home. Low-cost installations that omit permits often leave homeowners with compliance problems that cost more to resolve later. We handle all required permits and notifications as part of every installation.
