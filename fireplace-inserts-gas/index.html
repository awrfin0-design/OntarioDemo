Unlike an open wood-burning fireplace that loses most of its heat up the chimney, an insert captures and radiates heat into the room from the moment it is started.     Fireplace Inserts Gas Ontario Venting And Liner Work

    Every gas fireplace insert requires a venting system that runs from the insert to the exterior of the building. The most common approach for existing masonry chimneys is a flexible stainless steel liner that is lowered into the flue from above and connected to the insert at the firebox opening. Fireplace Inserts Gas Ontario from Ontario Service Pros includes the full liner installation as a standard part of every project, not a separate add-on to negotiate afterward.

    The liner serves two functions: it carries combustion gases out of the home and, for direct-vent models, brings fresh combustion air in through a coaxial sleeve. A properly installed stainless steel liner is rated to last 20 or more years under normal residential use when the insert is serviced annually. Around Ontario, Ontario Service Pros uses liner sizing specified by the insert manufacturer to ensure draft performance and combustion efficiency match the unit's design requirements.

    Direct Vent Versus B-Vent Options For Ontario Homes

    Direct vent inserts draw combustion air from outside and exhaust through a coaxial liner, making them the most efficient and safest choice for most residential installations. The sealed combustion system means no indoor air is consumed for burning and no backdraft risk exists even during high winds. B-vent inserts use indoor air for combustion and exhaust through a single flue liner, and while they suit some older-style setups, direct vent is the more common and recommended choice for Ontario properties receiving a new gas insert. Ontario Service Pros explains both options during the site assessment and recommends the right approach based on your fireplace configuration and home layout.

    Every gas fireplace insert project starts with a specific homeowner situation. Here are the ones we see most often and how we handle them.

    🏠

    Old Masonry Fireplace No Longer Used

    An open masonry fireplace that requires wood management, produces poor heat, or has an unreliable draft often goes unused for years despite taking up valuable wall space. A gas insert converts that opening into an efficient, on-demand heat source without structural changes to the room. We assess the firebox dimensions, select the right unit, and complete the full installation in a single organized visit.

    ❄️

    Cold Draft Coming Through the Firebox

    An unsealed masonry fireplace draws cold exterior air down the chimney and into the room even when not in use, making the space harder to heat during cold months. A gas insert seals the firebox completely, eliminating the cold-air pathway and the heat loss that comes with it. After installation, the draft disappears and the room holds heat more effectively throughout the heating season.

    🪵

    Wood Fireplace Too Much Work to Maintain

    Sourcing, storing, hauling, and stacking firewood takes time and physical effort that many homeowners no longer want to manage. A gas insert runs on the home's existing gas supply with no wood storage, no ash cleanup, and no waiting for a fire to build before the room warms. We handle the full conversion from the initial site assessment through to a tested, working system.

    🔥

    Fireplace Does Not Heat the Room

    Open masonry fireplaces lose most of their heat up the chimney because the firebox is not a sealed system, leaving the room barely warmer despite the fire burning. A gas insert replaces that open combustion chamber with a sealed, fan-assisted unit that directs heat into the room rather than up the flue. We size the insert to the room's square footage so the heat output is matched to the space from day one.

    🧱

    Chimney Damage Blocking Safe Use

    A chimney with cracked flue tiles, deteriorated mortar, or significant creosote buildup cannot safely support a wood fire, leaving the homeowner unable to use the fireplace at all. A gas insert with a properly installed stainless steel liner bypasses the damaged flue tiles entirely and creates a new, code-compliant venting path up the existing chimney. We assess the flue condition and complete the liner installation as part of the conversion project.

    📐

    Not Sure If the Opening Fits an Insert

    Firebox openings vary considerably in width, height, and depth, and many homeowners are unsure whether a gas insert will physically fit their specific masonry fireplace. We measure the opening precisely during the site assessment and match those dimensions to compatible unit models before any purchase is made. Our team confirms the fit first so there are no sizing surprises on installation day.

    🔧

    No Gas Line at the Fireplace

    Many homes with masonry fireplaces do not have a gas line nearby because the fireplace was originally built for wood use only. Our licensed gas technicians can run a new branch line from the home's existing gas supply to the fireplace location as part of the installation project. Every connection is pressure-tested before the insert is operated for the first time.

    🌡️

    Gas Insert Not Heating as Expected

    A gas insert that runs but does not warm the room effectively is usually undersized for the space, has a blocked burner reducing output, or has a blower that has failed so heat stays trapped inside the unit. We inspect the insert, confirm burner condition and gas pressure, and test the blower to identify which factor is limiting heat delivery to the room.

    ⚠️

    Gas Insert Pilot Won't Stay Lit

    A gas insert pilot that drops out shortly after lighting almost always has a worn thermocouple or thermopile that can no longer hold the gas valve open reliably. As the sensor degrades further, the fireplace becomes completely non-functional. We test sensor output, replace the failing component with the correct specification, and confirm stable pilot operation before leaving.
