    From ignition failures to heat pump faults, here are the issues homeowners near you call us about most.

    🔄

    Pool Heater Repeated Lockout After Reset

    A heater that resets and locks out again within minutes has an unresolved underlying fault, not a glitch. Common causes are a high-limit switch tripping from heat exchanger scaling, a flow sensor reading incorrectly, or a gas pressure issue preventing normal operation. We diagnose the actual trigger rather than repeatedly resetting the unit, and we repair the root cause so the lockout stops recurring.

    ❄️

    Pool Heater Running But Not Heating

    When the heater operates normally but the pool stays cold, the system is consuming energy without transferring it to the water. Gas units usually have a scaling problem in the heat exchanger, while heat pump units often have a low refrigerant charge or a fouled evaporator coil blocking airflow. We measure heat output against energy input to confirm which system is failing and correct it directly.

    🔥

    Pool Heater Won't Ignite at Start-Up

    A gas pool heater that clicks but will not light is usually dealing with a worn igniter, a fouled pilot assembly, or a gas valve not opening fully. Seasonal start-up after winter dormancy is the most common time for this to occur as moisture and inactivity degrade ignition components. We test the full ignition path from the control board through to the burner and replace whatever is confirmed faulty.

    💨

    Gas Smell at the Pool Heater

    Any detectable gas odour near the pool heater is a safety issue that requires immediate professional attention. Loose fittings, a cracking manifold, or a gas valve not sealing completely when off are the most common sources. Continuing to operate the heater in this condition creates a serious fire and explosion risk. We pressure-test all gas connections and valve seals and correct any leak before the unit is returned to service.

    ⚠️

    Error Code Showing on Pool Heater Display

    Pool heater error codes point to specific faults in the ignition sequence, safety switch circuit, or sensor readings. Reading the code without knowing what triggered it leads to misdiagnosis. We look up the fault against the manufacturer's diagnostic table, test the indicated components, and repair the part that is actually causing the error rather than just clearing the code and hoping it does not return.

    🌡️

    Heat Pump Not Reaching Pool Temperature

    A heat pump pool heater that runs continuously without reaching the target temperature is usually dealing with low refrigerant, a dirty evaporator coil reducing heat absorption, a failed capacitor causing the compressor to underperform, or cold ambient air temperatures limiting the unit's efficiency. We check all four factors systematically and correct the one causing the output shortfall.

    🔊

    Unusual Noise During Pool Heater Operation

    Banging sounds from a gas heater typically indicate delayed ignition, where gas accumulates before the flame lights. Rattling or vibration usually points to a loose component or a fan motor bearing wearing out on a heat pump. Neither noise should be ignored. We trace the sound to its mechanical origin, confirm whether it signals a safety issue, and make the repair before the fault progresses to a more expensive failure.

    🌱

    Pool Heater Not Starting After Winter

    Seasonal start-up failures are extremely common and usually involve a combination of igniter wear, a stuck gas valve, or a control board that needs resetting after extended shutdown. Cold temperatures and moisture during winter storage accelerate component degradation. We run a full seasonal start-up check covering ignition, gas pressure, safety switches, and flow confirmation to get the heater ready for the full season rather than just the first run.

    💧

    Pool Heater Water Leaking

    Leaks from a pool heater can originate from external inlet and outlet fittings, the pressure relief valve, bypass valve seals, or internal heat exchanger corrosion. Each source requires a different repair approach. We inspect all external connections first, then open the cabinet to trace internal leak sources systematically. After the repair, we pressure test the full circuit before closing the job.
