    Furnace Components That Fail When Cold-Weather Demand Peaks

    When a furnace runs hard through extended cold stretches, specific components face the most stress. Understanding the common failure points helps homeowners recognize when a service call cannot be deferred.

    • A cracked or worn igniter is the most frequent reason a furnace lights intermittently or fails to start entirely
    • A dirty or failing flame sensor causes the burner to light and then shut off within a few seconds, preventing sustained heating
    • A blocked or failing pressure switch stops the furnace from confirming proper venting before allowing the gas valve to open
    • Heat exchanger cracks are the most serious furnace fault, allowing combustion gases to mix with circulated air and creating a carbon monoxide risk
    • Blower motor failures prevent warm air from circulating even when the burner is operating correctly
    • A failed gas valve stops fuel delivery to the burner, leaving the system unable to produce any heat

    When a furnace fails in cold weather, the cause is almost always one of a handful of known issues. Here are the problems our clients call us about most.

    🔥

    Furnace Won't Start at All

    The most common cause is a cracked or burned-out igniter that can no longer reach the temperature needed to light the burner. Without ignition, the furnace runs through its startup sequence and shuts down without producing heat. We test the igniter first, confirm the control board is sending the correct signal, and replace the igniter with the right specification for your unit.

    🔦

    Burner Lights Then Shuts Off Right Away

    This almost always points to a dirty flame sensor coated in carbon residue, which prevents the sensor from confirming that the burner has lit. The furnace control board reads this as a failed ignition and shuts the gas valve within a few seconds as a safety measure. We clean or replace the flame sensor and test through a full start cycle to confirm stable burner operation.

    💨

    Furnace Running But No Heat Coming Out

    When the furnace appears to be running but rooms stay cold, the blower motor may have failed, preventing warm air from circulating through the ductwork. A failed gas valve can also cause this if the burner never ignites despite the system going through its startup sequence. We check both the air delivery side and the combustion side before confirming the cause.

    ⚠️

    Carbon Monoxide Alarm Going Off

    A carbon monoxide alarm triggered near the furnace should be taken seriously and can indicate a cracked heat exchanger allowing combustion gases to enter the living space. This is an immediate safety situation: evacuate the home and call for emergency service before re-entering. We perform a full safety inspection, confirm whether the heat exchanger is compromised, and explain the repair or replacement options before any further work proceeds.

    🔄

    Furnace Short Cycling On and Off

    A furnace that starts and shuts off repeatedly every few minutes is usually triggering the high-limit switch due to overheating, which is most often caused by a clogged air filter blocking airflow. Pressure switch failures from condensate line blockages and control board faults can also cause this pattern. We diagnose the specific trigger, clear any blockages, and test for complete cycle stability before finishing the call.

    🚨

    Gas Smell Near the Furnace

    A gas odour near the furnace indicates a potential gas leak in the supply line, at the valve connection, or within the unit itself. This is an immediate safety situation that requires shutting off the gas supply and leaving the home before calling a professional. Our licensed gas technicians locate the source of the leak, repair the affected section, and test the system for proper gas containment before restoring heating.

    📟

    Furnace Pressure Switch Fault Showing

    A pressure switch fault usually means the inducer motor is not creating the negative pressure needed to confirm safe venting before the gas valve opens. Common causes are a blocked condensate drain, a cracked inducer housing, or a failed pressure switch diaphragm. We check the entire venting and drainage path before testing the switch itself to avoid replacing a component that is not the root cause.

    🔊

    Furnace Making Loud Banging at Startup

    A loud bang when the furnace fires up is almost always a delayed ignition issue, where gas builds up in the combustion chamber before finally igniting all at once. This puts stress on the heat exchanger and can cause it to crack over time if left unaddressed. We adjust the ignition timing, clean the burners, and confirm the system lights cleanly on every startup cycle.

    📡

    No Heat and Thermostat Not Responding

    When the thermostat appears unresponsive and the furnace is silent, the issue is often a tripped furnace breaker, a blown fuse on the control board, or a communication fault between the thermostat and the unit. These are quick to diagnose but easy to misread as a major failure. We test the full control circuit from the thermostat through to the furnace board to find where the signal stops.
