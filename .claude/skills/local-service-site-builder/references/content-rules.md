# Content rules

The goal of every page: a customer in that place, with that problem, finds something they couldn't get from
the next company's site, and it's clear they should call this business.

## Page architecture

Create a page only when it has a distinct purpose and distinct content.

| Page | Create when | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Service page | The business sells it as its own job | Separate pages for keyword variants of one service ("furnace repair", "furnace fix", "fix my furnace", "furnace repairman"). That's one page. |
| Area page | You have ≥ 3 real local facts and something specific to say (how the job differs there, scheduling, common problems) | A city page whose only difference is the city name. City × service or city × brand matrices. |
| Brand page | The business is an authorised dealer/installer or has real depth with that brand | One page per brand that reads like every other brand page |
| Blog/guides | The owner can supply real expertise (costs, maintenance, seasonal advice) | Filler posts to "add content" |

Target the variants of a keyword inside the one page that covers it (title, H1, headings and body naturally
use the different phrasings). Use the Service Areas hub to list every community served. A list is the right
format for "we also go here".

## Writing each page

- **Lead with the customer's situation**, not the company: what they're seeing, what's likely wrong, what
  happens next.
- **Be specific.** Numbers, materials, times, prices, steps, local conditions. "Most main-line clears take 60–90
  minutes" beats "fast, reliable service".
- **Use the business's facts.** Their process, warranty, equipment, team, years. Ask if you don't know.
- **One topic per page.** The service page explains the service; it doesn't repeat the homepage.
- **Answer real questions** in the FAQ: price, timing, warranty, preparation, insurance, permits.
- **Show prices or ranges** where the business is willing. It's what customers want most and it differentiates.
- **Internal links**: link services ↔ related services, area pages → services, homepage → everything.
- **Headings**: one H1; H2s that a reader scanning the page would understand on their own.
- **Titles** 30–60 characters: `Service in Place | Brand`. Vary the wording naturally across pages.
- **Descriptions** 110–160 characters: what they get and why this business, not a keyword list.

## Banned

- Invented reviews, ratings, awards, certifications, licence numbers, years in business, team sizes or addresses
- Template sentences with the place name swapped in ("As a trusted provider in {city}, we…")
- Paragraphs copied between pages or between sites, including "rewritten" ones
- Keyword stuffing: lists of towns or keyword phrases in body text or footers
- Claims you can't back up: "best", "#1", "cheapest", guaranteed timelines the business doesn't offer
- Placeholder text, TODOs, lorem ipsum (the build fails on these)
- Medical, legal, safety or code claims without a source (e.g. gas, electrical, asbestos). State what the
  business does and refer to the authority.

## Making a series of sites distinct

When the same operator runs several sites in one industry, each site must stand on its own:

- Different business, different facts: each site belongs to a real, separate business or brand with its own
  phone, address/service area and Google Business Profile. Don't run multiple sites competing for the same
  queries in the same area for one business; consolidate into one strong site instead.
- Different angle: one company may lead with emergency response, another with fixed pricing, another with a
  speciality (heritage homes, commercial, eco upgrades). Pick the angle from the intake answers.
- Different structure: services, FAQs and sections differ because the businesses do. The design seed varies
  the visual layer on its own.
- Run `uniqueness_check.py` against every other site in the portfolio before each launch and after big edits.
