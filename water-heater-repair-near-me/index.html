    Most water heater failures follow the same patterns. Here are the issues local homeowners call us about most often and what we do to fix them.

    🔦

    Water Heater Pilot Light Won't Stay On

    A pilot that keeps going out is almost always caused by a worn thermocouple or thermopile, the components that tell the gas valve the pilot flame is active. When these sensors fail, the gas supply cuts off as a safety measure even when the pilot is trying to light. We carry these parts on every service vehicle and can replace them in most cases during the same visit.

    🔥

    No Hot Water Suddenly

    A sudden loss of hot water usually points to a pilot failure, a tripped thermal cutoff, a failed gas valve, or a control board that has stopped sending operating signals. Each of these produces a slightly different diagnostic pattern. We identify the actual source before quoting any replacement parts so the repair addresses the real cause.

    ⏱️

    Hot Water Running Out Too Fast

    A unit that cannot keep up with household demand has usually developed one of three problems: heavy sediment on the tank floor reducing effective capacity, a thermostat running below its original setting, or a gas valve not delivering a consistent fuel supply. We test for all three and correct whichever condition applies.

    🔊

    Water Heater Making Loud Sounds

    Rumbling, banging, or popping during a heating cycle almost always means sediment has accumulated on the tank floor and is trapping water beneath it. As that water heats, it forces through the sediment layer and makes the sounds homeowners notice. The buildup also insulates the burner and drives gas consumption higher over time. We flush sediment where it can still be removed effectively.

    💧

    Water Heater Leaking at Fittings

    Leaks at inlet and outlet connections, the pressure relief valve discharge, or the drain valve are usually caused by corrosion at the fittings or a component that has worn past its sealing capacity. These are repairable in most cases and should be addressed before the surrounding area sustains water damage. We identify the exact leak point and correct it rather than replacing parts that are not the source.

    🔴

    Hot Water Smells or Looks Off

    A sulfur or metallic smell from hot water taps is usually a sign that the anode rod has depleted and can no longer protect the tank lining. Rust-colored water on the hot side points to a corroding tank interior, which is a more serious condition. We assess both situations and explain clearly whether the unit can be serviced or whether replacement is the more practical path.

    ⚠️

    Water Heater Pressure Relief Valve Releasing

    A pressure relief valve that is dripping or releasing repeatedly is telling you the system pressure or temperature is exceeding safe operating limits. This is a safety signal, not just a minor annoyance. Common causes include thermal expansion, a thermostat set too high, or a valve seat that will not reseal after activation. We test the full pressure and temperature setup before recommending any repair.

    🚨

    Gas Smell Near the Water Heater

    A gas odour near the water heater should be taken seriously and never ignored or ventilated without also shutting off the gas supply. Common sources include a loose flex connector, a corroded fitting, or a gas valve allowing a small amount of unburned gas to escape. We inspect the full supply path from the shut-off to the burner and correct any leak points found during the visit.

    📅

    Water Heater Over 10 Years Old

    A gas water heater approaching or past the 10 to 12 year mark is entering the period when tank failure, anode depletion, and sediment accumulation all accelerate together. Continuing to repair an aging unit often costs more over two or three years than a proactive replacement. We assess the condition honestly and help homeowners decide whether repair or replacement is the better long-term investment.
