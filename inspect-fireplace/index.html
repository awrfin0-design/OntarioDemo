    Inspect Fireplace Ontario — What Our Technicians Check Step by Step

    When our technicians arrive for Inspect Fireplace Ontario service, they work through a structured inspection that covers every part of the appliance and its connected systems. The inspection is organized to move from the combustion zone outward through the venting path, so that any fault affecting air quality or safety is identified first.

    • Pilot light and ignition assembly: checks for consistent ignition, proper flame height, and clean sensor contact at the thermocouple tip.
    • Thermocouple and thermopile output: tests sensor voltage to confirm the gas valve is receiving a reliable signal to stay open during operation.
    • Gas valve function: verifies the valve opens and closes correctly, holds proper pressure, and responds to the safety controls as intended.
    • Firebox and heat exchanger: inspects for cracks, corrosion, scale buildup, and any physical damage that affects combustion quality or structural integrity.
    • Burner assembly and flame pattern: confirms even flame distribution across the burner, checks for blocked ports, and reviews combustion quality visually.
    • Venting path and draft: checks the flue or direct-vent run for obstructions, deterioration, and proper draft behavior during operation.
    • Glass and sealing: inspects the glass panel for cracks and reviews the seal condition around the combustion chamber door.
    • Safety shutoff controls and ODS sensor: confirms that the oxygen depletion sensor and manual shutoff function as designed.

    Once the inspection is complete, our technician walks the homeowner through every finding. Items that need attention now, items worth monitoring, and areas that are in good condition are each addressed clearly so the homeowner has a full picture of the appliance's status.

    Most fireplace faults develop gradually and show no obvious signs until they are serious. Here is what our technicians find most often during a professional inspection.

    🔥

    Pilot Light Starting to Fail

    A thermocouple that is losing output voltage will still hold the gas valve open most of the time, making the fault nearly invisible without testing. Eventually it causes repeated pilot outages or sudden shutdowns during use. We test sensor voltage during every inspection and replace thermocouples that are reading below the safe operating threshold.

    🔧

    Cracked or Corroded Heat Exchanger

    Heat exchanger damage typically develops from thermal cycling stress over several seasons and often shows no visible symptoms from the firebox opening. A cracked exchanger can allow combustion gases to mix with room air, creating a carbon monoxide concern during operation. We inspect the combustion chamber and exchanger surfaces carefully for any breach or deterioration.

    💨

    Venting Blocked or Deteriorated

    Vent blockages from debris, pest nesting, or collapsed liner sections prevent combustion gases from leaving the home correctly. Back-draft from a poorly drafting flue can introduce carbon monoxide and moisture into the living space. We check the full venting run and termination cap during every inspection to confirm the path is clear and structurally sound.

    💡

    Pilot Light Goes Out Repeatedly

    A pilot that requires frequent relighting is almost always caused by a dirty or mispositioned thermocouple, a pilot orifice clogged with dust or debris, or a gas supply pressure issue affecting the pilot flame. Ignoring this symptom can lead to the gas valve failing to open reliably over time. We clean and test the pilot assembly and confirm proper flame contact with the sensor tip.

    ⚠️

    Fireplace Keeps Shutting Off On Its Own

    Oxygen Depletion Sensor shutoffs are a safety response, not a malfunction. If the ODS is triggering frequently, it usually means the room ventilation is insufficient or the combustion quality has changed due to a dirty burner or venting restriction. We test ODS sensitivity and check combustion conditions to confirm whether the shutoff is appropriate or caused by a fixable underlying issue.

    🪟

    Fireplace Glass Cracked or Door Seal Worn

    Cracked fireplace glass is a safety hazard that allows heat and combustion gases to reach the surrounding area unexpectedly. A deteriorated door seal allows room air to enter the combustion chamber, affecting flame quality and efficiency. We inspect glass panels and door seals during every visit and note any damage that requires replacement before continued use.

    ⚠️

    Gas Smell During or After Operation

    A gas odour from the fireplace during operation or after shutdown can indicate a fitting leak, a valve not closing fully, or a connection that has shifted over time. This condition requires immediate evaluation by a licensed gas technician and the appliance should not be operated until the source is found. We use appropriate equipment to locate and resolve gas leaks safely.

    📅

    Fireplace Not Inspected in Over a Year

    A fireplace that has gone more than a year without a professional inspection may have sensor drift, vent deterioration, or component wear that is invisible from inside the room. The risk of a mid-season failure or carbon monoxide exposure increases with each season skipped. We provide documented findings after every inspection so homeowners have a clear record of system condition.

    🔶

    Yellow or Uneven Flame Pattern

    A gas fireplace that burns with a predominantly yellow or uneven flame is not burning cleanly. Common causes include blocked burner ports, a dirty burner assembly, or an air-to-gas ratio that has drifted. Left unaddressed, incomplete combustion increases carbon monoxide output and deposits soot on the glass and firebox walls. We clean the burner, check gas pressure, and confirm correct flame quality before completing the inspection.
